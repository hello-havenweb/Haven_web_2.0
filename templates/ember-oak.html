OUR CRAFTSMANSHIP

ROOTED IN FIRE.

At EMBER & OAK, we strip cuisine back to its elemental origin: dry oak firewood, iron grates, and seasonal ingredients harvested at peak character. We reject shortcuts, gas grills, and superfluous garnishes in favor of patient flame control and deep smoke integration.

Every log is hand-selected Oregon white oak, cured for twenty-four months to deliver clean, sweet smoke and sustained ember heat. We work directly with smallholder coastal farms, wild mushroom foragers, and heritage livestock growers to curate dishes that taste unequivocally of the landscape.

“Cooking over wood is a conversation with time. The flame demands your full attention, and in return, gives food an indelible soul.”

THE EXPERIENCE

Every detail of an evening at EMBER & OAK is orchestrated for unhurried presence and sensory depth.

01

THE FIRE

Open-flame cooking and oak-fired preparation. Our hearth burns constantly from noon to midnight, generating a live bed of glowing embers that imparts nuanced smokiness without overwhelming pure flavor.

02

THE TABLE

Warm interiors, handcrafted materials, and intimate dining. Reclaimed dark walnut tables, hand-thrown ceramic plateware, heavy hand-cut glassware, and soft flickering candlelight welcome you to settle in.

03

THE EVENING

Slow meals, thoughtful service, and memorable moments. We never turn tables hastily. Your reservation belongs to you for the duration of your courses, paired with cellar selections and attentive care.

THE STEWARD OF THE HEARTH
CHEF ADRIAN VALE
Executive Chef • Founder
“We cook with fire because it leaves something behind — character.”

Following twenty years leading Michelin-acclaimed kitchens across Copenhagen, San Francisco, and the Pacific Northwest, Adrian Vale founded EMBER & OAK to return to the primal essence of hospitality.

“Modern cooking often hides behind equipment. Over wood embers, you cannot cheat. The heat responds to wind, moisture, and grain. When you master that balance, the flavor has a resonance that no oven can imitate.”
